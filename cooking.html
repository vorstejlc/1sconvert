Cooking measurements explained

US recipes measure by volume (cups and spoons), while most other countries weigh ingredients in grams. Cup sizes also differ between countries:

  • US cup: 236.6 mL (often rounded to 240 mL on nutrition labels)
  • Metric cup (UK, Australia, New Zealand, Canada): 250 mL
  • Tablespoon: 14.8 mL (US) or 15 mL (metric). Australian tablespoon: 20 mL
  • Teaspoon: 4.93 mL (US) or 5 mL (metric)

Converting cups to grams depends on the ingredient: a cup of flour weighs about half as much as a cup of sugar. Use the table below for common ingredients. Related: weight converter, volume converter and temperature converter.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams is a cup of flour?

One US cup of all-purpose flour is about 120 g, spooned and levelled. Scooping straight from the bag packs it down and can give 140 g or more.

How many grams is a cup of sugar?

One US cup of granulated sugar is about 200 g. Packed brown sugar is about 213 g, and powdered (icing) sugar about 113 g.

How many tablespoons are in a cup?

There are 16 tablespoons in a US cup, and 48 teaspoons. A metric 250 mL cup is about 16.7 metric tablespoons.

Is a UK cup the same as a US cup?

No. Recipes from the UK, Australia and New Zealand use a 250 mL metric cup. A US cup is 236.6 mL, about 5% smaller.

How many grams of butter are in a stick?

One US stick of butter is 4 oz, 113 g or 8 tablespoons. One cup of butter is 2 sticks, or 227 g.

What is 180 °C in Fahrenheit for baking?

180 °C is 356 °F, usually written as 350 °F (gas mark 4) in recipes. 200 °C is about 400 °F (gas mark 6).
