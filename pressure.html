How to convert pressure units

Pressure is force per unit area. The SI unit is the pascal (Pa), 1 N/m². It is very small, so engineering uses kPa, MPa and bar (1 bar = 100 kPa, close to atmospheric pressure). The US uses psi, pounds-force per square inch. Medicine uses mmHg for blood pressure, and weather maps use hPa or millibars, which are the same thing.

  • bar to psi: multiply by 14.5038
  • psi to bar: multiply by 0.0689476
  • psi to kPa: multiply by 6.89476
  • kPa to psi: multiply by 0.145038
  • atm to bar: multiply by 1.01325
  • mmHg to kPa: multiply by 0.133322

Gauge vs absolute: tyre and refrigeration gauges read pressure above atmospheric (psig, bar g). Absolute pressure adds about 1.013 bar (14.7 psi) at sea level. Related: refrigerant PT chart and force converter.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert bar to psi?

Multiply bar by 14.504. For example, 2.4 bar = 34.8 psi and 10 bar = 145 psi.

How do I convert psi to bar?

Multiply psi by 0.06895, or divide by 14.504. For example, 32 psi = 2.21 bar.

What is 1 atmosphere in other units?

One standard atmosphere is exactly 101,325 Pa: 101.325 kPa, 1.01325 bar, 14.696 psi or 760 mmHg.

What is the difference between psig and psia?

psig is gauge pressure, measured relative to the surrounding air. psia is absolute pressure, measured from a perfect vacuum. At sea level, psia = psig + 14.7.

Are hPa and mbar the same?

Yes. One hectopascal (hPa) equals one millibar (mbar) = 100 Pa. Standard sea-level air pressure is 1,013.25 hPa.

Are mmHg and Torr the same?

They are almost identical. 1 Torr is 1/760 of an atmosphere (133.322 Pa), and 1 mmHg is 133.322 Pa. The difference is negligible for everyday use.
