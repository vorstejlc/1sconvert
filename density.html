How to convert density units

Density is mass per unit volume. The SI unit is kilograms per cubic metre (kg/m³). Laboratories usually work in grams per cubic centimetre (g/cm³), which is the same as g/mL, kg/L and tonnes per m³. US engineering uses pounds per cubic foot (lb/ft³).

  • g/cm³ to kg/m³: multiply by 1,000
  • kg/m³ to g/cm³: divide by 1,000
  • lb/ft³ to kg/m³: multiply by 16.018
  • kg/m³ to lb/ft³: multiply by 0.062428
  • lb/in³ to g/cm³: multiply by 27.68

Specific gravity (relative density) is a material's density divided by the density of water. Because water is about 1,000 kg/m³, a specific gravity of 0.8 means about 800 kg/m³, or 0.8 g/cm³.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert g/cm³ to kg/m³?

Multiply by 1,000. For example, aluminium at 2.70 g/cm³ is 2,700 kg/m³. To go the other way, divide kg/m³ by 1,000.

How do I convert lb/ft³ to kg/m³?

Multiply pounds per cubic foot by 16.018. For example, 62.4 lb/ft³ (water) is about 1,000 kg/m³, and 150 lb/ft³ (concrete) is about 2,400 kg/m³.

What is the density of water?

Pure water is about 1,000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³ or 62.4 lb/ft³). It is densest at 4 °C (999.97 kg/m³) and slightly lighter at 20 °C (998.2 kg/m³). Seawater is about 1,025 kg/m³.

Is g/mL the same as g/cm³?

Yes. One millilitre is exactly one cubic centimetre, so 1 g/mL = 1 g/cm³ = 1 kg/L = 1,000 kg/m³.

What is the difference between density and specific gravity?

Density has units, such as kg/m³. Specific gravity has no units: it is the ratio of a material's density to that of water. Steel has a density of about 7,850 kg/m³ and a specific gravity of about 7.85.
