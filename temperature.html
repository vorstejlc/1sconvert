Temperature conversion formulas

Unlike most units, temperature scales have different zero points, so you cannot convert with a single multiplication.

  • Celsius to Fahrenheit: °F = °C × 9/5 + 32
  • Fahrenheit to Celsius: °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9
  • Celsius to Kelvin: K = °C + 273.15
  • Fahrenheit to Rankine: °R = °F + 459.67

Quick mental estimate: to get °F, double the Celsius value and add 30. For 20 °C that gives 70 °F; the exact value is 68 °F. The shortcut is close between about 0 and 30 °C.

A temperature difference converts without the offset: a change of 1 °C (or 1 K) equals a change of 1.8 °F. Related: oven temperatures and the refrigerant PT chart.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?

Multiply by 9/5 (or 1.8) and add 32. For example, 25 °C × 1.8 + 32 = 77 °F.

How do I convert Fahrenheit to Celsius?

Subtract 32, then multiply by 5/9. For example, (350 °F − 32) × 5/9 = 176.7 °C.

At what temperature are Celsius and Fahrenheit equal?

−40 °C is exactly −40 °F, the only point where the two scales meet.

What is normal body temperature in Fahrenheit?

37 °C is 98.6 °F. A fever is usually taken to start around 38 °C (100.4 °F).

What is absolute zero?

Absolute zero is 0 K, which is −273.15 °C or −459.67 °F. It is the lowest possible temperature.

Why is Kelvin written without a degree sign?

Kelvin is an absolute scale and an SI base unit, so it is written as K, not °K. A difference of 1 K equals a difference of 1 °C.
