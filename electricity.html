Electrical units and prefixes

This converter covers five electrical quantities: voltage (volts, V), current (amperes, A), resistance (ohms, Ω), charge (coulombs, C, and amp-hours, Ah) and capacitance (farads, F). Within each quantity you are only moving between SI prefixes, such as milliamps to amps or kilohms to ohms.

You cannot convert directly between quantities, such as volts to amps. You need a second value and Ohm's law:

  • V = I × R: voltage equals current times resistance
  • I = V ÷ R or I = P ÷ V: current from resistance or from power
  • P = V × I: power in watts. For example, a 2,000 W kettle on 230 V draws about 8.7 A.

Related: power converter (W, kW, hp) and energy converter (Wh, kWh).

Frequently asked questions

How many milliamps are in an amp?

There are 1,000 milliamps (mA) in one amp (A). To convert mA to A, divide by 1,000: 250 mA = 0.25 A.

How do I convert mAh to Wh?

Multiply milliamp-hours by the voltage, then divide by 1,000: Wh = mAh × V ÷ 1,000. A 10,000 mAh power bank at 3.7 V holds 37 Wh.

Can I convert volts to amps?

Not directly, because they measure different things. You also need the power or the resistance: amps = watts ÷ volts, or amps = volts ÷ ohms. For example, 1,000 W at 230 V is about 4.35 A.

How do I convert μF to nF?

Multiply microfarads by 1,000. 0.1 μF = 100 nF = 100,000 pF, which is the value marked 104 on a ceramic capacitor.

How many ohms are in a kilohm?

One kilohm (kΩ) is 1,000 ohms, so 4.7 kΩ = 4,700 Ω. One megohm (MΩ) is 1,000,000 Ω.

How many coulombs are in an amp-hour?

One amp-hour is 3,600 coulombs: 1 A flowing for 3,600 seconds. One milliamp-hour is 3.6 C.
